# Changelog

What changed in each version of Playbook.

## 0.5.5 (2026-11-13)

The first public version of Playbook, an offline team comp planner for VALORANT that runs from a single HTML file.

- The Planner ranks the comps that fit your situation. Give it the map, side, sites, rank and party size, plus any agents you have to play or won't play, teammates who've locked in, the enemy team's agents and how many of each role you want. You can also ask for the best map, side or rank for a comp. Each result gets a fit score out of 100 and a tier, and shows how it wins, its pros and cons, what sets it apart from the top pick, plays to run on the minimap and tips. Each reason names the ability that does the job it describes, so Sage slows a push with Slow Orb and Jett’s Tailwind is her way out after an Operator shot. A comp’s pros each make their own point. Once one names Sova’s Recon Bolt, the rest move on to something else. When a con says who brings a strength, it names everyone who does, so Sage’s Slow Orb counts as crowd control and Fade’s Prowler as a flash. A con says nobody brings a strength only when nothing in the comp does, so a team whose only crowd control is Killjoy’s Lockdown hears that it comes from an ultimate most rounds don’t have. An agent whose fights come from an ultimate, like Veto’s Evolution, isn’t praised for winning plenty of fights alone. A con about a pick that does less on the map, or two picks that get in each other’s way, says how to play around it, and no pro credits that pick with what the con says the map takes away. No pro quotes an agent’s win rate on a map where their team lost most of its games. Playing solo, in a duo or in a trio, you hear first about your own agents, with at least one pro about each of them. An agent you won’t play can still turn up as a random teammate’s likely pick, and the comp says so. A comp warns that nobody plays a role only where that costs the team, so a solo player’s random team isn’t told it needs an initiator, since random teams without one win as often as any. Against a known team, a comp says how it stands against each threat. Nothing in a kit counts as an answer to a Sova or Fade finding your push, so the advice is to shoot down or step out of what finds you and hit together, and from Platinum up to show one site and hit the other. Against lineups that guard a planted Spike, the comp hears which of its utility holds up the plant or speeds up the retake. A comp is told it’s short of Operator play only on maps with long sightlines, and below Gold only on Breeze. A con about an extra sentinel says what it costs on the side you asked about, and below Platinum a defense isn’t told off for a second one, since locking sites down wins rounds there. Below Platinum, no reason on a card sells fakes.
- Advice changes with your rank. From Iron to Radiant, each answer weighs what a team at that rank gets out of every kit, using ranked win rates and, mostly from Diamond up, pro picks. When an answer says how many pro teams on a map run a role, the share comes from what they played at this year’s events. Icebox isn’t in this year’s pro map pool, so its pro picks come from Masters Toronto 2025, the last top event to play it, and agents released since then count neither for nor against there. Agents that are hard to play well count for less at lower ranks, and a comp with one says so, even when it’s an agent you chose to play. Neon, Astra, Yoru and Hush take the most practice, so the best comps leave them out until Platinum, apart from Neon on an open map’s attack at Gold. Leaving Phoenix out doesn’t bring Hush in either. Breach waits until Silver, since his stuns and flashes need teammates who follow up. Phoenix does less on open maps like Abyss and Breeze, where his flash and molly reach few fights. Two controllers pay off only for a team that plays its smokes together, so a comp with two counts for a little less until the top ranks. A duo or trio that plays both of them smokes together as well as a five-stack at its rank, so it weighs the pair the way that five-stack would, and its random teammates aren’t expected to lock in a third.
- Every one of the 13 maps has its own plays. There are hits, splits, fakes, defaults, setups, stacks, pushes and retakes for every site, plus pistol and eco rounds. Each play is filled in with your comp and replays on a minimap you can zoom and drag. Nobody is asked for more smokes or flashes than their abilities hold in a round, so Omen flashes one corner with his single Paranoia and the play says which corner goes without. Below Platinum, a comp’s first play is one that doesn’t need groups timed to the second. With no site given, a defense starts from a setup that covers every site whenever it fits as well as a stack. From Platinum up, a defense asked about one site starts from a retake setup or an early push there, with the stack on it later in the list. You can draw your own plays too.
- Agent select suggests who to lock in as your team picks. Rankings has tier lists for any map, side and rank. Every agent and map has its own page.
- Saved keeps your plans and notes, your usual parties, the plays you draw and the games you log, all in your browser, with backups to a file.
- Light and dark themes (or your own), settings for text size, motion and sound, full keyboard support, undo for every change, and a guided tour of each page.
